CODE THE VOICE

The 3-Day AI Voice Agent Bootcamp
Build an AI that listens, thinks and talks back.

Siri, Alexa and Google Assistant were built by large teams of engineers. In three days, your students will build their own voice agent.

Every team builds it twice: first on ElevenLabs, the no-code platform that businesses use to launch voice agents, and then in Python with Pipecat, the open-source framework engineers use in production. The finished agent holds a natural conversation, answers from its own knowledge base and books appointments on a calendar. On the last day, it takes a live call in front of the school’s teachers.

2. Why This Bootcamp

  • Voice AI is where the industry is heading. Banks, hospitals, recruiters and logistics companies now use AI agents to answer and make calls. Students learn the technology behind them.
  • Students use real industry tools. ElevenLabs and Pipecat are used to build voice agents for actual businesses, not just for classrooms.
  • It covers both paths. Students first build without code to understand design, then build in code to understand what happens underneath.
  • It stretches students who can already code. The finished agent is a strong project for science exhibitions, hackathons and college applications.

3. What Students Will Build

Every team builds a voice agent on the cascade architecture, the design used in most commercial voice agents:

LISTENVoice activity detection and speech-to-text
THINKLanguage model, system prompt and knowledge base
ACTTools such as calendar booking and a database
SPEAKText-to-speech in a natural voice

Students build this pipeline twice: once on ElevenLabs without code, and once in Python with Pipecat.

Each team chooses a real use for its agent, for example a school helpdesk, a clinic appointment desk, a college admission enquiry line or an exam revision tutor.

6. Learning Outcomes

By the end of the bootcamp, students will be able to:

  1. explain where voice AI agents are used and why businesses are adopting them;
  2. describe the cascade and speech-to-speech architectures and choose between them;
  3. build a no-code voice agent with a structured prompt, knowledge base, workflows, tools and guardrails;
  4. build a code-based voice pipeline in Python with Pipecat, including function calling;
  5. deal with real-world problems such as delay, turn-taking, interruptions and background noise;
  6. test an agent, improve it and present it as a product.

7. What Students Take Home

  • The design of their no-code agent: its system prompt, workflow and knowledge base, so that they can build it again later.
  • The complete Pipecat project code, with comments.
  • A bootcamp handbook that explains every module step by step.
  • A certificate of completion from BGIEM. The top three teams also receive awards.
  • Four weeks of online doubt support through a Google Classroom managed by the school.

8. Student Safety and Data Privacy

  • No student creates an account on any AI service. Teams work in workspaces managed by BGIEM.
  • The keys used in the Pipecat module are temporary and limited, and are switched off after the bootcamp.
  • Every agent has guardrails, so that it stays on its topic and refuses unsafe requests.
  • Test calls use sample details only. Calendar booking uses a BGIEM demo calendar and a sample database, never real personal data.
  • Photographs are taken only as per the school’s policy.

9. Cost to the School

The bootcamp is offered free of cost. BGIEM bears all expenses, including the trainers, platform accounts, materials, certificates and awards. The school only provides its computer lab for the three days.
