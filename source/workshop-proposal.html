Workshop Proposal

AI Innovation Lab: Build Your Own AI

A hands-on workshop for students of Classes 8 to 12, proposed to {{SCHOOL_NAME}}, Jabalpur

1. About the Institute

Baderia Global Institute of Engineering & Management, Jabalpur, was established in 2009. It is approved by AICTE and affiliated to Rajiv Gandhi Proudyogiki Vishwavidyalaya (RGPV), Bhopal. The Institute offers undergraduate, postgraduate and diploma programmes in engineering and management, including B.Tech in Computer Science and Engineering with specialisation in Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning. Through its school outreach programme, the Institute conducts workshops that introduce school students to new technologies through practical work.

2. Need for the Workshop

  • AI has become a part of school education. CBSE offers Artificial Intelligence as a subject, and students are expected to understand how AI systems work, not only how to use them.
  • Most students use AI chat apps but rarely see what is inside an AI system: the data, the model, the prompt and the code. Building one from start to finish makes these ideas clear.
  • Voice assistants and computer vision are two of the fastest-growing areas of AI. Building small versions of both gives students a practical start in these fields.
  • Working in a team on a real project builds coding, problem-solving and presentation skills, and gives students a project they can show in exhibitions and future applications.

3. Objectives

  1. To give students practical experience of building a working AI application.
  2. To explain the core ideas of AI, such as training data, models, prompts and APIs, through hands-on work.
  3. To improve students’ coding and logical thinking through a real project.
  4. To develop the habit of using AI safely and responsibly.
  5. To introduce students to careers in computer science and Artificial Intelligence.

5. Track A: Build Your Own Voice AI AgentFor students of Classes 11 and 12

Each team builds a voice assistant that runs in the web browser. The assistant listens to the user, understands the request, fetches live information when needed and replies in a natural voice, in English or Hindi. Students start from starter code provided by BGIEM and add one capability at a time. They learn the same architecture that is used in commercial voice agents.

How the assistant works

PartWhat it doesTechnology used
ListenConverts the user’s speech into textWeb Speech API in Google Chrome
ThinkDecides how to respondRules written in JavaScript, and then a large language model (Google Gemini) through BGIEM’s lab server
ActFetches live information when neededFree public APIs: Open-Meteo for weather and Wikipedia for facts
SpeakReads the reply aloudSpeech Synthesis API in the browser

Build levels

LevelWhat students buildConcepts learnt
1. Voice loopAn assistant that listens through the microphone and speaks back what it heardSpeech-to-text, text-to-speech, browser APIs and events
2. Rule-based brainReplies to fixed commands such as the time, the date, a timer and school informationIntents, conditions and string matching in JavaScript
3. ToolsLive answers from the internet: the weather of any city and short facts from WikipediaAPIs, HTTP requests, JSON and asynchronous code
4. AI brainA large language model connected through the BGIEM lab server, with a system prompt that gives the assistant a name, a role and limitsLarge language models, system prompts and guardrails
5. Advanced featuresMemory of the conversation, switching between English and Hindi, and stopping speech when the user interruptsContext, state and turn-taking
6. Demo DayEach team presents an assistant built for a purpose of its choice, such as a school helpdesk, an exam tutor or a city guideProduct thinking and presentation

6. Track B: Sign2SpeakFor students of Classes 8 to 10

Each team trains its own AI model to recognise hand signs using the webcam, and then builds a web app that speaks the recognised sign aloud, for example “Namaste”, “Thank you” or “Help”. The project shows how AI learns from data and how a trained model is used inside an application. It also has a social purpose, since a tool of this kind can help people with speech or hearing difficulties.

Build levels

4. Sign2Speak appA web app that shows the camera, the predicted sign and a confidence bar, and speaks the word aloudUsing a model in code, confidence threshold and text-to-speech

7. Learning Outcomes

After the workshop, students of Track A will be able to:

  • explain how a voice assistant works, from speech input to spoken reply;
  • use browser APIs and public web APIs in JavaScript, and handle JSON data;
  • connect an application to a large language model and control it with a system prompt and guardrails.

Students of Track B will be able to:

  • collect training data and train an image recognition model;
  • test a model for accuracy and bias, and improve it with better data;
  • use a trained model inside a working web application.

Students of both tracks will understand the basic rules of safe and responsible use of AI.

8. What Students Take Home

  • Their complete working project: the source code of the voice assistant (Track A), or the model link and the app (Track B).
  • A lab manual with step-by-step instructions, so that they can continue the project at home or in the school lab.
  • A certificate of participation from BGIEM. The top three teams in each track also receive awards.

11. Student Safety and Data Privacy

  • No student creates an account on any AI service, and no API key is given to students. The AI brain in Track A works through BGIEM’s lab server, which has usage limits.
  • Every assistant built in Track A is given guardrails in its system prompt, so that it stays on its topic and refuses unsafe requests.
  • Speech recognition in Google Chrome is processed by Google’s online speech service. Students are told not to share personal information with their assistants.
  • In Track B, the training images stay on the lab computer. Only the trained model is uploaded when a team exports it, and the camera is pointed at the hands.
  • Photographs are taken only as per the school’s policy.
  • Each session ends with a short discussion on the responsible use of AI.

12. Assessment, Certificates and Reporting

  • On Demo Day, a panel of school teachers and the BGIEM trainer judges each team out of 100 marks: working project (30), use of AI concepts (25), creativity of the idea (20), code quality or model accuracy (10) and presentation (15).
  • All participants receive certificates, and the top three teams in each track receive awards.
  • Within three working days, BGIEM shares a workshop report with the school. It includes attendance, photographs, the list of projects and a summary of student feedback.

13. Follow-up After the Workshop

  • The lab manual and starter code are handed over to the school’s computer teachers, so that the practicals can be repeated with other classes.
  • For four weeks after the workshop, students can get their doubts cleared online through a Google Classroom managed by the school.
  • The best teams are invited to an inter-school AI showcase at the BGIEM campus, where they present their projects and visit the Institute’s labs.
