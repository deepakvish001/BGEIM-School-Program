1. The Idea

Most AI sessions in schools end with students having watched a few impressive demonstrations. This workshop is planned the other way round. Students spend almost the entire time at the computers, working in small teams, and by the end of three hours every team has built and presented an AI project of its own.

Each team works like a young start-up. It picks a real problem from its school or from Jabalpur, studies the problem with AI, builds a working app for it, designs a logo and a poster, plans an automation and finally pitches the whole idea at a Demo Day in front of its teachers. Every AI concept is learnt while doing one of these tasks, at the moment the students need it, rather than in a lecture before it.

By the end of the workshop, every team will have:
  1. A machine learning model, trained by the team, that sorts waste and runs on a phone
  2. A research notebook on its problem, with a mind map and an AI-made podcast
  3. A working web app with a shareable link and a QR code
  4. A logo, a poster and a pitch slide for its project
  5. An automation plan and an 8-second AI promo video
  6. A Demo Day pitch, certificates and a take-home AI Starter Kit

2. What Makes This Workshop Different

  • Practical from the first minute. About 80 percent of the time is spent on the computers, not on slides.
  • Learning by building. Students learn what training data, bias, prompting and automation mean by actually doing them.
  • Local and real. The problems come from the students' own school and city, such as waste on campus, the Narmada, traffic at the school gate and the heritage of Jabalpur.
  • Something to show at home. Every team leaves with working links that open on any phone.
  • Built to continue. The school receives a lab manual, worksheets and a four-week follow-up plan, so the work does not stop when the workshop ends.
  • Safe by design. School-managed logins, no personal accounts, and no faces or personal data used in training.

4. Mission-wise Lab Plan

Each mission is written in the form of a lab practical so that the same sheets can later be used by the school’s computer teachers.

Opening: Can AI Read Your Doodles?

10 minutes
AimTo break the ice and show that AI learns from examples.
ToolsQuick, Draw! (quickdraw.withgoogle.com), projector
Procedure
  1. Teams play Quick, Draw! for two minutes and try to make the computer guess their drawings.
  2. The trainer explains how the game guesses: it has learnt from millions of doodles drawn by people around the world.
  3. “Real or AI?”: six images are shown on screen and teams vote on each one.
  4. Each team picks a problem card (Section 5) and chooses a team name.
Concept learntAI learns patterns from data.
Wow momentThe computer recognises a half-finished drawing within seconds.

Mission 1: Teach a Machine

25 minutes
AimTo train an image recognition model and understand how the training data affects the result.
ToolsTeachable Machine (teachablemachine.withgoogle.com, no login needed), webcam, waste items brought by the trainers: plastic bottles, paper and fruit peels or leaves
Procedure
  1. Open Teachable Machine and start a new Image Project (Standard image model).
  2. Create three classes: Plastic, Paper and Organic.
  3. Hold each item in front of the webcam and record 50 to 100 images per class from different angles.
  4. Click Train Model and test the model live with the items.
  5. Bias test: show the model an item it has not seen before, such as a bottle of another colour or a crumpled sheet of paper. Note where it fails, add more varied images and train it again.
  6. Click Export Model and then Upload my model. Turn the shareable link into a QR code and open the model on a phone.
OutputA working waste-sorting model with a shareable link.
Concept learntTraining data, classes, accuracy, bias, and why varied data matters.
Wow momentA model the team trained ten minutes ago recognises waste on a phone.

Mission 2: Research Like a Scientist

20 minutes
AimTo study a problem from trusted sources using AI, and to check every answer through its citation.
ToolsNotebookLM; a source pack for each problem (three or four articles or PDFs, prepared in advance by the trainers)
Procedure
  1. Create a new notebook and add the team’s source pack.
  2. Start an Audio Overview in English or Hindi first, as it takes a few minutes to generate.
  3. While it is being made, ask three questions about the problem and open the citation for each answer.
  4. Generate a mind map of the topic.
  5. Listen to the first minute of the podcast and write the three most important facts on the team worksheet.
OutputA research notebook, a mind map and an AI podcast on the team’s problem.
Concept learntSource-grounded AI, citations and fact-checking.
Wow momentTwo AI voices discussing the team’s own topic as a podcast.

Mission 3: Prompt Battle

15 minutes
AimTo learn how to write good prompts through a friendly competition.
ToolsGemini, Nano Banana (in the Gemini app), prompt card, class leaderboard
ProcedureThree rounds of about five minutes each. Teams write their prompts, the trainer runs the best few on the projector and the class votes.
  • Round 1, Explain It: make Gemini explain photosynthesis so that a Class 3 child understands it, in exactly five lines.
  • Round 2, Format Master: get a table of five Indian states with their capital, main language and one famous dish, using a single prompt.
  • Round 3, Recreate the Image: a picture is shown on screen, and teams try to recreate it as closely as possible with Nano Banana.
OutputPoints on the leaderboard, and the R-T-C-F prompt card (Annexure B).
Concept learntRole, Task, Context and Format, and how clear instructions change the result.
Wow momentOne extra line in a prompt completely changes what the AI produces.

Mission 4: Build the App

35 minutes
AimTo build and share a working web app for the team’s problem without writing code by hand.
ToolsGemini with Canvas; Lovable, shown by the trainer as the advanced option
Procedure
  1. Fill in the app plan on the worksheet: name of the app, who it is for, and three features.
  2. Turn on Canvas in Gemini and write the build prompt using the template on the prompt card.
  3. Test the app in the preview and note one thing that needs fixing.
  4. Improve the app in three rounds: add a feature, fix a problem, improve the design.
  5. Add the footer “Made by Team ___ at the BGIEM AI Innovation Lab”.
  6. Share the app, make a QR code for the link and open it on a phone.
OutputA working web app, such as a quiz, a planner, a checklist or a calculator, with a shareable link.
Concept learntHow AI writes code, why testing matters, and why logic is still needed.
Stretch challengeTeams that finish early try to connect their Teachable Machine model to the app to make a “smart bin” page.
Wow momentAn app built in half an hour, running on a teacher’s phone.

Mission 5: Brand It

20 minutes
AimTo design the identity and the pitch material for the team’s project.
ToolsNano Banana (Gemini app), Gemini Canvas
Procedure
  1. Write the 8-second promo video prompt first and hand it to the co-trainer, who starts generating the video in Google Flow.
  2. Create a logo for the app and improve it with two follow-up instructions.
  3. Create an A4 poster with the app’s name, a tagline and space for the QR code.
  4. Make a single pitch slide covering the problem, the solution, how it works and the team members.
OutputLogo, poster, pitch slide and video prompt.
Concept learntText-to-image generation, editing images with words, and labelling AI-made content.
Wow momentA professional-looking poster created in a few minutes.

Mission 6: Automate It

10 minutes
AimTo understand automation and AI agents.
ToolsTeam worksheet; Google Workspace Studio (demonstration by the trainer)
Procedure
  1. Each team plans one automation for its project in the form Trigger → Steps → Result. For example: when someone fills our feedback form → save the answers in a Sheet → send a thank-you email.
  2. Two teams read out their plans.
  3. The trainer builds the best plan live in Google Workspace Studio and runs it in front of the class.
OutputAn automation plan on the worksheet.
Concept learntTriggers, steps, AI agents, and the need for human supervision.
Wow momentA teacher fills the form on a phone and the email appears on the screen a moment later.

Demo Day and Closing

35 minutes
AimTo present the project with confidence and to close the workshop with a commitment to safe use of AI.
FormatEach team gets two minutes: the problem, a live demo of the app on the projector, the poster, and the premiere of its AI promo video. The panel consists of the principal or teachers nominated by the school, and the BGIEM trainer.
JudgingUsefulness of the idea (25), working app (25), use of AI tools (20), design and poster (15), presentation (15). Total: 100 marks.
AwardsBest AI Innovation, Best App, Best Prompt Engineers (from the Mission 3 leaderboard), Best Design, and Teachers’ Choice.
ClosingAll students read the AI Safety Pledge together, receive certificates and the AI Starter Kit, and pose for a group photograph.
Wow momentEach team sees its own AI-made promo video on the big screen for the first time.

6. Take-home AI Starter Kit

Every team receives a digital folder, shared through a QR code, containing:

  • the link to its Teachable Machine model and to its web app;
  • its NotebookLM notebook and AI podcast;
  • its logo, poster, pitch slide and AI promo video;
  • e-certificates for every team member.

Every student also receives a printed R-T-C-F prompt card, the AI Safety Pledge and the 30-Day AI Explorer Challenge:

WeekChallenge
1Retrain the waste model with three new classes (glass, metal and e-waste) and test it at home.
2Make a NotebookLM notebook for one school subject and revise one chapter with its quiz and flashcards.
3Add two new features to the team’s app and share it with the class.
4Teach one family member how to use AI safely, and write a short note on what you learnt.

7. After the Workshop: Continuation Plan

7.1 Teacher Handover

A 30-minute session for the school’s computer teachers, held on the day of the workshop or a day before. Teachers receive the lab manual (the mission sheets in Section 4), the account setup guide, the problem cards and the worksheets, so that they can repeat the practicals with other classes.

7.2 AI Explorers Club (Four Weeks)

One 60-minute lab session every week, run by the school’s computer teacher with a BGIEM mentor:

WeekThemeLab work
1Better data, better modelsSound and pose projects in Teachable Machine; improving model accuracy
2AI for examsA NotebookLM study notebook for each subject, with quizzes and flashcards
3App upgradeNew features, testing with classmates, and publishing the final version
4Showcase preparationFinal pitch, poster and video for the inter-school showcase

7.3 Inter-school AI Showcase at BGIEM

The best teams from both schools present their projects at the BGIEM campus, visit the Institute’s engineering labs and receive awards. This gives students a real audience for their work and a first look at engineering education.

7.4 Mentoring

Follow-up questions are handled through a Google Classroom set up and managed by the school, with BGIEM mentors added as guest teachers.

9. Safety and Responsible Use

  • No student creates a personal account during the workshop; only school-managed or BGIEM-managed logins are used.
  • No faces or personal data are used to train models. Mission 1 uses only objects.
  • Photographs of students are taken only as per the school’s permission policy.
  • Every AI-made image, poster and video is labelled as AI-generated.
  • Facts used in student apps are checked from reliable sources before the apps are shared.
  • The workshop ends with the AI Safety Pledge: check before you trust, keep personal information private, use AI to learn and not to copy, respect others, and follow age rules.
